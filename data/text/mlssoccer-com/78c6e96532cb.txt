Australia's 2026 FIFA World Cup run came to a heartbreaking end on Friday. Still, rising Colorado Rapids defender Lucas Herrington left the tournament with the backing of two of the sport's most iconic players.

Following Australia's penalty-kick defeat to Egypt in Friday's Round of 32 showdown at Dallas Stadium, FOX analysts Thierry Henry and Zlatan Ibrahimović praised Herrington for stepping up to take a penalty during the shootout.

Although Herrington put his attempt off the crossbar, both legendary strikers lauded the courage the 18-year-old center back showed in the match's defining moment.

"Don’t be upset; you go there taking it, you miss. I feel for you, but keep your head up," said Henry, who featured at four World Cups with France during his illustrious playing career.

"I'm thinking about the guy that allowed him to go," continued the former Red Bull New York star. "You should \[say\], 'No, this is not on you. We're going.' So, fair play to \[Herrington\]. I know you missed, but we'll see you again."

Ibrahimović, known as one of the best strikers of his generation, expressed a similar sentiment.

"Listen, in this moment, I said to you, penalty is like lottery. You score, you become a hero. You don't score, sadly, you become zero," said the legendary Swedish international.

"But I just want to reach out to Herrington," continued the former LA Galaxy star. "You're 18 years old, you're young, this is just the beginning of your career. You stepping up there, you showed a lot of courage. Not everybody will do that. My friend: You're the best. Don’t listen to the rest.

"... It's not easy to step up there to shoot that penalty, and it's a moment of seconds," Ibrahimović added. "Either it goes well, or it goes not. But listen, Herrington. If you want to reach out, I'm here."

Herrington has emerged as one of MLS's brightest young prospects and a breakout performer for both club and country over the last year.

The Colorado defender quickly earned a starting role with the Rapids, parlaying that success into a spot on Australia's World Cup roster.

He carved out a significant role for Australia, becoming the youngest-ever <a href="https://x.com/MLS/status/2070329958078075364" target="_blank">MLS</a> and <a href="https://x.com/Socceroos/status/2070325450887635075" target="_blank">Socceroos</a> player to start a World Cup game. Herrington was in the starting XI twice during Australia's run to the knockout rounds, including Friday's contest against Egypt.

Even with the Round of 32 defeat, Herrington has elevated his profile during the tournament, with increased European interest anticipated following his World Cup showing.

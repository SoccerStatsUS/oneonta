. <br>Throughout the two-week program, players were introduced to Nationwide’s corporate culture as they met with leaders in marketing, social media, communications, corporate sustainability, talent acquisition, diversity, equity and inclusion and many other areas of the enterprise. <br>“We’ve learned so much in these two weeks,” said Shea Groom. “We got to see so many career opportunities that exist outside of playing soccer, and working with a company like Nationwide who sponsors and is passionate about the NWSL has really furthered my professional knowledge beyond the pitch.” <br>The five players had a busy schedule of immersive opportunities, including media training, appearances on Nationwide’s podcasts and a tour of Nationwide Children’s Hospital in Columbus, where they learned how several Nationwide-sponsored events support the hospital and patient families. <br>Players also explored Nationwide’s full sports marketing portfolio, meeting virtually with NFL partners before making in-person visits to the NHL’s Columbus Blue Jackets and MLS’ Columbus Crew facilities, where they met with front office staff from both organizations, including former players. <br>

<br>“Hats off to Nationwide for putting together this program and recognizing an area of need for professional athletes,” said former MLS player Dante Washington, now the Columbus Crew’s Director of Team Strategic Partnerships and Business Development. “For these athletes to use their time to come to a city they’ve probably never been to before, to start preparing for life after their playing careers are over is very commendable, and I encourage more athletes to do that.” <br>“Being an athlete is a huge advantage in business,” said Columbus Blue Jackets Team Relations Specialist Aaron Johnson, a former player for the club. “These players know what it’s like on the field and can bring their experience to the business side and make improvements. As athletes, we continuously work and find ways to improve and we’re really good problem solvers, which helps make the transition from playing to an office role.” <br>

<br>The players also spent a day volunteering at the Mid-Ohio Food Collective, a local food bank that provides over 170,000 meals each day to 20 counties in Ohio. This experience highlights the importance of community impact, the core of the NWSL and Nationwide’s relationship. <br>“People look up to us as role models and leaders, and one way that they can learn from us is by giving back,” said Cassie Miller. “So many people have given me so much and I love to give back.

The NWSL season may be on summer break, but many of the league’s biggest names are still playing soccer. A whopping 138 NWSL players – including youth national team players – have been called up to represent their countries during the FIFA window.

(Updated June 5)

138 total call-ups (including youth national teams and alternates), the most in one window in NWSL history

All 16 NWSL clubs have at least one player called up

34 countries represented

35 players called into youth national team camps, including the U.S. (U-18, U-19, U-20, U-23) as well as England U-20, Mexico U-23, and Sweden U-23 squads

52 players named to U.S. Women’s National Team and youth national team rosters, including June friendlies against Brazil

The USWNT is back in action playing a long-time rival: Brazil. It’s a rematch of the gold medal game at the Paris Olympics and an early measuring stick as preparations for the 2027 FIFA Women’s World Cup continue and this time, it’s on Brazilian soil.

The June roster is packed with NWSL talent, including familiar veterans like Rose Lavelle, Emily Sonnett, Trinity Rodman, Mallory Swanson, and Sophia Wilson, while younger players like Claire Hutton, Avery Patterson, Lilly Reale, and Croix Bethune continue pushing for bigger roles.

Brazil, meanwhile, will feature plenty of NWSL stars of its own. Marta, Angelina, Rafaelle, Dudinha, Ludmila, Gabi Portilho, Lorena, Ary Borges, and Amanda Gutierres are among the league players expected to feature for the hosts.

In South America, the CONMEBOL Liga de Naciones Femenina will wrap up this month. The tournament serves as South America’s World Cup qualifying competition, with the top two teams – outside of host nation Brazil – automatically advancing to the World Cup. Right now, those top two teams are Argentina and Colombia but Venezuela is hot on their heels.

Washington Spirit midfielder Leicy Santos is currently tied for the tournament scoring lead with four goals through April play. She’s been on a tear in the NWSL and she’s playing just as well for Colombia, giving NWSL fans another reason to tune in to the final matchdays of the tournament. And if that wasn’t enough incentive, fellow league NWSL players like Daniela Arias, Claudia Martínez, and Aldana Cometti are also expected to play key roles for their countries.

Across Europe, NWSLers have been playing major roles for their national teams as they seek 11 highly sought-after FIFA Women’s World Cup spots. UEFA qualifying groups are made up of three to four teams competing home and away.

These matches will be the final group-stage matches, with the group leaders automatically qualifying for the World Cup. Playoffs will begin in October and end in December, determining who will earn the final seven spots.

No club has more international representation this month than the Washington Spirit.

The Spirit have players representing Canada, Colombia, Côte d’Ivoire, England, Italy, Mexico, Nigeria, Paraguay, Scotland, and the United States, showcasing one of the deepest international rosters in the league.

Gotham FC, Portland Thorns FC, Seattle Reign FC, and San Diego Wave FC are also heavily represented throughout the June window.

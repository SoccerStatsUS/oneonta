TELL OF THE TALE

<br>The 2022 NWSL Shield-winners OL Reign put together a remarkable 22-game regular season run that culminated with a 3-0 win over the Orlando Pride on the last day to earn the trophy. The first-place finish is just the club’s third since 2014 (2014, 2015, 2022) and put them in the driver’s seat for the playoffs.

<span>NUMBERS TO KNOW</span><br>40 – the number of points needed for OL Reign to clinch the 2022 NWSL Shield, and a number they achieved on the final day with a 3-0 win over the Orlando Pride. The 40 points earned are still two lower than they achieved last year (42 points from 24 games – 2 <sup>nd</sup> place) but count as the third time since 2018 that the side has hit that mark (2018 – 41 points from 24 games – 3 <sup>rd</sup> place). <br>11 – the number of wins achieved by OL Reign this season, one more than any other team in the league (four with 10 wins). In their conquests, there are still four teams that the Reign faced twice this season that they did not beat (Chicago, Racing, Portland, Washington). However, the Reign also then pulled the double on four sides (Orlando, Gotham, North Carolina, Angel City) to help achieve the win total they did. <br>3 – the number of consecutive seasons that forward Bethany Balcer has led her side in goals, with her and forward Megan Rapinoe sharing the scoring crown this year with seven goals each. Balcer also led the team in 2019 and 2021, becoming just the second player in the league to front their team’s scoring charts every regular season since 2019. <br>1 – the number of players to feature in all 22 regular season games, which is goalkeeper Phallon Tullis-Joyce. Tullis-Joyce managed a league-high nine clean sheets to tie Portland netminder Bella Bixby for the lead in that category, and only midfielder Olivia Van der Jagt (21 GP) and forward Veronica Latsko (20 GP) played in 20 or more games. <br><span>TELL OF THE TALE</span> <br>The 2022 NWSL Shield-winners OL Reign put together a remarkable 22-game regular season run that culminated with a 3-0 win over the Orlando Pride on the last day to earn the trophy. The first-place finish is just the club’s third since 2014 (2014, 2015, 2022) and put them in the driver’s seat for the playoffs. <br>A somewhat wacky start the season ensued first, with the Reign failing to win any of their opening four games (D3 L1), before back-to-back wins over Kansas City and San Diego at the end of May kickstarted their campaign. A drier June saw the Reign only pick up one win in their three games, but a better July (W2 D1 L1) helped to regulate the side a bit more. <br>After picking up just five points in a five-game stretch from July 10 to August 7, though, the Reign upped their pace drastically the rest of the season beginning with back-to-back wins over Gotham and Orlando throughout the rest of the month of August. September then saw the Reign open the month with an exciting 2-2 draw against Chicago that required a comeback from 2-1 down after going 1-0 up. From there, the side ran out the rest of the season with four consecutive wins over North Carolina (A), Gotham (A), Houston (A) and Orlando (H) to tick up the season tally and claim the Shield. <br>Now, OL Reign faces the Kansas City Current in the NWSL semifinals after a bye week via the first-place finish, with the clash scheduled for Sunday, October 23. <br><span>QUOTED</span> <br><u>OL Reign goalkeeper Phallon Tullis-Joyce</u> : <br>On her impression of OL Reign’s regular season … <br>“It has been a whirlwind of a season honestly, so many emotions. I think even just with the Challenge Cup, we as a team really set the tone of what we expected of ourselves this season and to carry out in the regular season as well. Props to this league because wow, it created such a wild season for every team involved, especially for it to come down to the last game. It was really cool to be a part of that and a part of a league that is truly so strong that you don’t know who’s going to end up coming out at the top spot at the end of the day. But really happy to be able to clinch the Shield and it was a wild ride, but I was really happy to do that with my teammates, our staff, and our fans, who were amazing throughout the season. <br>On her season as a whole… <br>“Coming in having joined the team about halfway through the season last year, I came in in the starting position this season. I had started in France as well so I did have the professional experience there playing in bigger games, especially against the likes of Lyon and PSG. Now coming into the NWSL, playing against so many amazing teams, for myself, I am happy with my performance and happy with how I’ve grown with the team. I’m very thankful for our coaching staff and my goalkeeper coach and all the work he put in to put me in this spot to perform. I’m always my biggest critic so I can’t wait to continue working on myself and this league forces you to learn so much so quickly, especially as a goalkeeper. If anything, I’m just really excited after it because it exposes you right off the bat. Anything you’ve got going on, you can’t hide anything in this league (laughs). I’m excited to keep working on those things.” <br>On how the Reign kept focus through the season… <br>“We are a team that is fortunate to have many national team players, whether that be the U.S. National Team or so many others. We really have so many players going to different countries when we are in those FIFA breaks that it’s incredible. It speaks to the depth of our team even during those breaks that the those who came in the team and held it down was just as good and it speaks to the work ethic of our team. We really do harp on the word relentless, we’re in it and are about to grind it out until the finish and that’s what we saw with clinching the Shield. We did have some tough times and some tough games where results didn’t go our way but, it’s probably scary being those opponents right after those games because we’re right on it again.” <br>On playing in front of the atmosphere at Lumen Field… <br>“It was really cool that we were able to play in Lumen (Field) this year, especially being able to see our fans. Especially after the games, our fans are just incredible. They have banners for us, our Royal Guard is always showing out for us and we have the Academy teams that come and show up—it’s really just such a special atmosphere afterwards. We really do feel their presence during the games and we appreciate them so much and can’t wait to see them on Sunday (against Kansas City).” <br>

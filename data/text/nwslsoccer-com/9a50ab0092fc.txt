It’s been a long offseason, but the wait is over. The NWSL kicks off its 2026 season on Friday, March 13, and with the league now expanded to 16 teams, there’s more action than ever before.

Simply put: we’ve never been more back.

In any given week, with the NWSL one of the most competitive leagues in the world, you never know who will come out on top, and that’s never more true than in Week 1, with its unlimited possibilities. Who will need to shake off the rust? Who will surprise everyone? Who is looking to be the hero for their team?

In the NWSL, it’s anyone’s game.

What better way to kickoff the season than a Rowdy Audi playoff rematch? For the Thorns, 2026 starts where 2025 ended. The Spirit triumphed over the Thorns in the semifinals to advance to the NWSL Championship, but it’s a new season. None of that matters now.

Home field advantage is on a different level in Washington D.C. After Trinity Rodman’s record-breaking re-signing with the Spirit over the offseason, expect the crowd to cheer her on loudly and proudly. Rodman’s return will only help to energize an offense that found its rhythm in the 2025 postseason. If Rosemonde Kouassi and Gift Monday can pick up where they left off late last year, the Spirit’s frontline could once again be one of the most dynamic in the league. Forward Leicy Santos remains a reliable scoring threat, while a back line anchored by 2025 Defender of the Year Tara Rudd and the ever-composed Rebeca Bernal gives Washington balance on both sides of the ball. Although on maternity leave at the moment, veteran striker Ashley Hatch and Andi Sullivan, fresh off of SEI, will round out a team that just missed lifting the trophy last season.

Portland, meanwhile, arrives with several intriguing storylines of its own. Sophia Wilson has already seen her first game action after returning from maternity leave, in a friendly versus C.F. Monterrey last weekend, and her presence instantly elevated the Thorns’ attack. Midfielder Olivia Moultrie continues to grow into a central role, while Portland’s youth movement – featuring Pietra Tordin and Reilyn Turner – adds speed and unpredictability to the squad. The Thorns will also lean on experienced contributors like Reyna Reyes and Deyna Castellanos as they adjust under a new head coach hired just before the season began.

While both sides of this matchup have strong defenses, (hello, 2025 Defender of the Year Tara Rudd!), they’ll be without longtime veteran presences in goal. With Aubrey Kingsbury on maternity leave, Mackenzie Arnold on international duty, and Bella Bixby on SEI, it’ll be both a test and opportunity for the second and third string keepers. However it unfolds, NWSL fans will have a season opener to sink their teeth into.

The newest installment in Boston-New York rivalries is here. Going against the reigning NWSL Champions is a tall task for the newcomers, but what better way to cut your teeth? With a mix of NWSL veterans like Ella Stevens, Casey Murphy, and Nichelle Prince setting the tone and international players like Amanda Gutierres and Aïssata Traoré ready to make an impression, it’s a clean slate for the Legacy. Coach Filipa Patão will make her NWSL debut at the helm, and while there are many questions about how the Legacy’s inaugural season will pan out, the return of women’s professional soccer to Boston is a win already.

And for Gotham, their quest for back-to-back championships begins on the road. Gotham players like Emily Sonnett and Jaedyn Shaw made a splash for the U.S. Women’s National Team at the SheBelieves Cup, and if that international performance is any indication, Gotham will be ready to roll for the first 90 minutes of the season. Keeping much of the championship roster intact, Gotham’s has only added depth with exciting rookies Andrea Kitahata and Jordynn Dudley. Add the return of Tierna Davidson from SEI into the mix and Gotham has the potential to pick up right where they left off.

The Kansas City Current were electric in 2025, and they managed to maintain much of that roster. The depth of the Current roster is a spoil of riches – and that’s even with stars Temwa Chawinga and Michelle Cooper starting the season on SEI. Adding Croix Bethune and Penelope Hocking into the mix with threats like Debinha and Ally Sentnor has potential for some fun and creative play. The reigning Goalkeeper of the Year, Lorena, is reliable as ever anchoring the defense that only allowed 13 goals in 2025.

On paper, the Current make for a near-impossible team to beat, but soccer is played on the pitch and the Utah Royals enter 2026 with something to prove. It helps to have a player like Mina Tanaka at the front. In her second NWSL season, Tanaka found her groove, scoring six goals but showing a hunger for more. After winning just one game in the first half of the season, the Royals found a rhythm – which included a win over the Portland Thorns at Providence Park. With exciting signings like Kiana Palacios, there is renewed excitement coming to the Utah front line.

The NWSL’s second newest team, Denver Summit, plays its first official match on the road as San Jose’s Bay FC opens its season at its home, PayPal Park, site of the 2025 NWSL Championship. After a disappointing 2025, where they came in 13th place after making the playoffs in their inaugural season, Bay FC has a lot of ground to cover in 2026. Offseason acquisitions like Italian Cristiana Girelli and Argentine captain Aldana Cometti bring needed veteran experience and Florida State rookie defender Heather Gilchrist brings energy and a championship mindset on the backline but it’s USWNT forward Claire Hutton who is the darling of Bay’s recent maneuverings. In fact, the offseason was pretty busy for the club. Emma Coates joins as the new head coach and the roster was reshaped with both veteran additions and a blockbuster trade as of March 11. Bay sent 2025 team-leading scorer Penelope Hocking to the Kansas City Current for $350,000 in intra-league funds, signaling a new direction for the attack.

Denver’s squad looked good in their preseason scrimmages and are already playing like they have a season together under their belts already. They may not have Lindsey Heaps yet but they have plenty of other pieces to give Bay FC issues, and a few of them are homegrown. Bay Area native and Stanford grad Jasmine Aikey is already getting Rookie of the Year buzz. Add that with NWSL veterans Kaleigh Kurtz – who has played every single minute of every regular-season match since the start of the 2022 season – and Janine Sonis and the Summit are sure to give every opponent an uphill battle (pun intended).

The North Carolina Courage host Racing Louisville on Saturday night in a matchup between two teams that largely stood pat this offseason, trusting the cores that carried them through 2025. For North Carolina, that core nearly delivered a playoff berth. The Courage finished just a point shy of the postseason despite a standout campaign from Midfielder of the Year Manaka Matsukubo, whose creativity powered much of the club’s attack. Now under new head coach Mak Lind, the Courage will look to turn last year’s near miss into a return to the playoffs.

Racing Louisville, meanwhile, arrives in Cary coming off the most successful season in club history. Under reigning NWSL Coach of the Year Bev Yanez, Louisville reached the playoffs for the first time in club history, clinching their spot on Decision Day with a win over Bay FC that simultaneously knocked the Courage out of contention. Although Racing’s postseason run ended in a dramatic penalty shootout loss to the Washington Spirit at Audi Field, the experience proved the club has the pieces to compete deep into the playoffs. USWNT forward Emma Sears, who has been electric with the national team since November, will again be a focal point of Louisville’s attack alongside midfield leader Taylor Flint.

One of the most notable changes in this matchup comes between the posts. North Carolina signed Kailen Sheridan in free agency after Casey Murphy departed for expansion side Boston Legacy, bringing in the NWSL’s all-time leader in penalty kick saves to anchor the back line. On the other side stands Racing’s Jordyn Bloomer, who stepped into the starting role last season and delivered several heroic performances throughout Louisville’s playoff push. With two athletic shot-stoppers and two teams capable of generating chances through their established cores, Saturday night’s contest may ultimately come down to which goalkeeper can fly further—and faster—when it matters most.

The San Diego Wave parted ways with Kailen Sheridan and Delphine Cascarino this offseason, but quickly retooled their attack by bringing in Brazilian internationals Ludmila and Gaby Porthilo. Kenza Dali and Ludmila will challenge a Houston defense anchored by veteran goalkeeper Jane Campbell and defender Avery Patterson.

This marks Campbell’s 10th season with the Dash. She ranks among the league's best shot-stoppers, having passed a 15,000 minutes played milestone at the end of last season, but has yet to see the Dash claim the title of NWSL Champion. This year, after signing two of the top collegiate forwards – Kate Radar from Duke and 2024 MAC Hermann winner Kate Faase from UNC – this could very well be their year.

And one of the most wholesome storylines emerging from opening weekend could very well happen in San Diego. It’s not every day a player has a chance to make her professional debut. It’s even rarer still for that player to make it while facing off against her sister. That could be the case for San Diego rookie Mimi Van Zanten, who could make her league debut on Sunday against her older sister, Houston’s Kiki Van Zanten. It should make for a fun moment during Saturday’s game and one that NWSL fans who love trivia should pin in their memory banks.

After a record-setting Shield and Championship-winning season in 2024, the Pride went out early, seeing their title-defending campaign end in the seminals. In the offseason, they lost a few key pieces of that special 2024 squad, with Kylie Nadaner now out on maternity leave and Carson Pickett and Ally Brazier (née Watt) moving to Denver and Emily Sams to Los Angeles, and while they added Hallie Mace to bolster the defense, the creative burden offensively will fall to Jacquie Ovalle, Barbra Banda, and Marta, the emotional and spiritual heartbeat of the club.

Seattle, meanwhile, enters Sunday’s matchup riding the momentum of a quietly impressive 2025 campaign. After missing the playoffs in 2024, a postseason appearance and stats that were commendable, the squad this year looks more recharged than ever. 2025 NWSL Goalkeeper of the Year nominee Claudia Dickey returns in goal and the Reign’s youth scoring contingent – Emeri Adames, Maddie Dahlien, Ainsley McCammon, and (checks notes) defender Jordyn Bugg – compliment some of their more experienced veteran teammates Jess Fishlock and Sofia Huerta. The Reign also welcome a full season of Mia Fishel after she arrived late last year, giving Seattle another dangerous option in the final third. With neither team making any major changes to the roster, this match may be determined by who looks sharper in front of goal.

Angel City opens the new season in a bit of a transition period after saying goodbye to two of the most recognizable figures in club history, Ali Riley and Christen Press. But the club enters 2026 re-energized, with plenty of intriguing storylines to tell. Riley Tiernan returns after a strong rookie campaign in which she showed a knack for finding the back of the net while defender Savy King makes her long-awaited return to the field after missing much of last season due to a frightening medical emergency. Los Angeles also bolstered its backline in the offseason, adding 2024 Defender of the Year Emily Sams, alongside a new class of rookies. With dynamic defender Gisele Thompson and the creatively technical Japanese internationals Hina Sugita and Jun Endo returning, this may be a squad that looks ready to make postseason runs.

Chicago enters the season hoping to turn the page on a disappointing 2025 at the bottom of the table. The Stars will have to do so without one of last season’s most electric attackers, the backflipping Brazilian Ludmila, who now calls San Diego home. And while Mal Swanson is making her return from maternity leave, the retooled attack now includes two arrivals from Houston – Michelle Alozie and Ryan Gareis. Chicago also strengthened its backline, adding one of the best youth defenders in her generation, 2025 ACC Defender of the Year and All-American Elise Evans from Stanford. With both sides looking to change their fortunes in the new season after an offseason of transition and introspection, Sunday is the perfect opportunity for fans to see what they’ve come up with.

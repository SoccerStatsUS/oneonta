It’s been a big week for Gotham. They made history with 42,175 fans in the stands at Citi Field, where Sam Kerr made her return and Rose Lavelle scored the first of her two game-winning goals in Matchweek 13.

Big as those moments are, Gotham Coach Juan Carlos Amorós knows some things are more important than what happens on the pitch, and he’s leading by example. In the wake of Gotham’s two-match week, Amorós is stepping away from the club for five weeks of paternal leave following the birth of his second child.

Amorós said he's doing what was best for him and his family but he also saw taking leave in his position as an opportunity to be an example for others.

“This is a personal decision for our family, but I also recognize the visibility that comes with my role,” Amorós said in a press conference. “When fathers take paternity leave, particularly men in leadership positions, it helps normalize an important principle. Caring for children is a shared responsibility between parents, and I need to be there for my family.”

The moms in the league who have given birth have no choice but to step away from the game to physically rest and recover, but all parents – whether they give birth or not – have a right and need to bond with their children. With more parents in the NWSL now than ever, it’s becoming even more normal for players to take leave.

That push for parental leave in the NWSL, which Amorós credited players like Midge Purce for helping lead, has changed what it means to be a parent in the league.

“Childcare is a parental issue,” Amorós said. “This is a value I believe in for myself, for my wife, and for my family. I’m deeply appreciative of our players supporting me in this decision as well. That is very important to me. They’ve been fantastic.”

While Amorós is on leave, lead assistant coach Shaun Harris will serve as acting head coach for Gotham.
